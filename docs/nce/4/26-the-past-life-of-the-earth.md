# 第 26 课：The Past Life of the Earth

> [新概念英语 · 第四册 流利英语](/nce/ "返回目录")

The past life of the earth<br><span class="zh">地球的往昔生命</span>

What is the main condition for the preservation of the remains of any living creature?<br><span class="zh">保存任何生物遗骸的主要条件是什么？</span>

It is animals and plants which lived in or near water whose remains are most likely to be preserved,<br><span class="zh">最有可能被保存下来的，是那些生活在水中或水边的动植物的遗骸。</span>

for one of the necessary conditions of preservation is quick burial,<br><span class="zh">其中一个必要的保存条件就是迅速掩埋。</span>

and it is only in the seas and rivers, and sometimes lakes,<br><span class="zh">而只有在海洋、河流中，有时也在湖泊里，</span>

where mud and silt have been continuously deposited,<br><span class="zh">在那些泥沙不断沉积的地方……</span>

that bodies and the like can be rapidly covered over and preserved.<br><span class="zh">尸体等物才能被迅速覆盖并保存下来。</span>

But even in the most favourable circumstances<br><span class="zh">但即使在最有利的情况下</span>

only a small fraction of the creatures that die are preserved in this way<br><span class="zh">只有极少数死亡的生物能够以这种方式被保存下来。</span>

before decay sets in or, even more likely, before scavengers eat them.<br><span class="zh">在腐烂开始之前，或者更可能的是，在食腐动物吃掉它们之前。</span>

After all, all living creatures live by feeding on something else,<br><span class="zh">毕竟，所有生物都是通过摄取其他物质来维持生存的。</span>

whether it be plant or animal, dead or alive,<br><span class="zh">无论是植物还是动物，无论是死的还是活的……</span>

and it is only by chance that such a fate is avoided.<br><span class="zh">只有碰巧才能避免这样的命运。</span>

The remains of plants and animals that lived on land are much more rarely preserved,<br><span class="zh">生活在陆地上的植物和动物的遗骸被保存下来的情况要少得多。</span>

for there is seldom anything to cover them over.<br><span class="zh">因为很少有东西能将它们覆盖起来。</span>

When you think of the innumerable birds that one sees flying about,<br><span class="zh">当你想到那些在空中飞舞的无数鸟类时……</span>

not to mention the equally numerous small animals like field mice and voles which you do not see,<br><span class="zh">更不用说那些同样数量众多但你看不到的小动物，比如田鼠和鼩鼱。</span>

it is very rarely that one comes across a dead body, except, of course, on the roads.<br><span class="zh">人们很少能碰到动物的尸体，当然，路上除外。</span>

They decompose and are quickly destroyed by the weather or eaten by some other creature.<br><span class="zh">它们会腐烂，很快被风化或被其他动物吃掉。</span>

It is almost always due to some very special circumstances that traces of land animals survive,<br><span class="zh">几乎总是由于一些非常特殊的情况，陆地动物的痕迹才能得以保存下来。</span>

as by falling into inaccessible caves, or into an ice crevasse,<br><span class="zh">比如掉进无法进入的洞穴里，或者掉进冰裂隙里。</span>

like the Siberian mammoths,<br><span class="zh">就像西伯利亚猛犸象一样……</span>

when the whole animal is sometimes preserved, as in a refrigerator.<br><span class="zh">有时整个动物会像在冰箱里一样被保存下来。</span>

This is what happened to the famous Beresovka mammoth which was found preserved and in good condition.<br><span class="zh">这就是著名的贝列索夫卡猛犸象的遭遇：它被发现时保存完好，状态极佳。</span>

In his mouth were the remains of fir trees--the last meal that he had before he fell into the crevasse and broke his back.<br><span class="zh">它嘴里还残留着冷杉树枝——那是它掉进冰缝摔断背前的最后一餐。</span>

The mammoth has now been restored in the Palaeontological Museum in St.Petersburg.<br><span class="zh">这头猛犸象现已被修复并陈列在圣彼得堡的古生物博物馆中。</span>

Other animals were trapped in tar pits, like the elephants, sabre-toothed cats,<br><span class="zh">还有些动物陷进了沥青坑，比如大象、剑齿虎</span>

and numerous other creatures that are found at Rancho la Brea, which is now just a suburb of Los Angeles.<br><span class="zh">以及在兰乔拉布雷亚发现的众多其他生物，那里如今只是洛杉矶的一个郊区。</span>

Apparently what happened was that water collected on these tar pits<br><span class="zh">显然，发生的情况是水在这些沥青坑中积聚了。</span>

and the bigger animals like the elephants ventured out on to the apparently firm surface to drink,<br><span class="zh">那些体型较大的动物，比如大象，便冒险走到了那片看起来非常坚固的地面上去喝水。</span>

and were promptly bogged in the tar.<br><span class="zh">结果立刻陷进了沥青里。</span>

And then, when they were dead, the carnivores,<br><span class="zh">然后，当它们死去之后，那些肉食动物……</span>

like the sabretoothed cats and the giant wolves,<br><span class="zh">就像剑齿虎和巨型狼一样……</span>

came out to feed and suffered exactly the same fate.<br><span class="zh">它们出来觅食，结果遭遇了完全相同的命运。</span>

There are also endless numbers of birds in the tar as well.<br><span class="zh">沥青里还有数不清的鸟类的遗骸。</span>
