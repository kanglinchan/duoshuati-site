# 第 31 课：The Sculptor Speaks

> [新概念英语 · 第四册 流利英语](/nce/ "返回目录")

The sculptor speaks<br><span class="zh">雕塑家之言</span>

What do you have to be able to do to appreciate sculpture?<br><span class="zh">要真正欣赏雕塑，你需要具备哪些条件或能力呢？</span>

Appreciation of sculpture depends upon the ability to respond to form in 3 dimensions.<br><span class="zh">对雕塑的欣赏，取决于人们能否理解和回应三维空间中的形态（即雕塑的立体结构与形态特征）。</span>

That is perhaps why sculpture has been described as the most difficult of all arts;<br><span class="zh">也许正因为如此，雕塑才被人们称为所有艺术形式中最难掌握的一种。</span>

certainly it is more difficult than the arts which involve appreciation of flat forms, shape in only two dimensions.<br><span class="zh">当然，这比那些只需要欣赏二维平面形状的艺术形式要困难得多。</span>

Many more people are 'form-blind' than colour-blind.<br><span class="zh">有'形式盲'的人比有色盲的人多得多</span>

The child learning to see, first distinguishes only two-dimensional shape; it cannot judge distances, depths.<br><span class="zh">正在学习视力的孩子最初只能识别二维的形状；他们还无法判断距离或物体的深度。</span>

Later, for its personal safety and practical needs, it has to develop (partly by means of touch) the ability to judge roughly 3-dimensonal distances.<br><span class="zh">后来，为了自身安全和实际需要，它必须（部分通过触觉）培养出大致判断三维距离的能力</span>

But having satisfied the requirements of practical necessity, most people go no further.<br><span class="zh">然而，在满足了实际需求之后，大多数人就不会再进一步去追求更高的目标了。</span>

Though they may attain considerable accuracy in the perception of flat form,<br><span class="zh">尽管他们在识别平面形状方面可以达到相当高的准确度</span>

they do not make the further intellectual and emotional effort needed to comprehend form in its full spatial existence.<br><span class="zh">他们没有付出进一步的智力与情感努力，去全面理解“形式”在其空间存在中的本质（即形式在三维空间中的具体表现方式）。</span>

This is what the sculptor must do.<br><span class="zh">这就是雕塑家必须做的事情。</span>

He must strive continually to think of and use, form in its full spatial completeness.<br><span class="zh">他必须不断努力去思考和运用形式在空间中的完整性</span>

He gets the solid shape as it were, inside his head--he thinks of it, whatever its size, as if he were holding it completely enclosed in the hollow of his hand.<br><span class="zh">他在脑海中清晰地想象出那个物体的形状；无论这个物体的实际大小如何，他都仿佛能够将其完全握在手中一样。</span>

He mentally visualizes a complex form from all round itself;<br><span class="zh">他在脑海中从各个角度想象出一个复杂的形体</span>

he knows while he looks at one side what the other side is like; he identifies himself with its centre of gravity, its mass, its weight;<br><span class="zh">他看着一面时就知道另一面是什么样子；他体会其重心、质量和重量</span>

he realizes its volume as the space that the shape displaces in the air.<br><span class="zh">他认识到这个物体的“体积”，其实就是该物体在空气中占据的空间（即该物体将空气“挤开”后所形成的空间）。</span>

And the sensitive observer of sculpture must also learn to feel shape simply as shape, not as description or reminiscence.<br><span class="zh">对于雕塑而言，敏锐的观察者也必须学会纯粹地将形状视为形状本身来感知它，而不是将其视为某种描述或回忆的象征。</span>

He must, for example, perceive an egg as a simple single solid shape quite apart from its significance as food,<br><span class="zh">例如，他必须将鸡蛋仅仅视为一个简单的、独立的固体形状，而忽略其作为食物的意义。</span>

or from the literary idea that it will become a bird.<br><span class="zh">也不把它看作会变成鸟的文学意象</span>

And so with solids such as a shell, a nut, a plum, a pear, a tadpole, a mushroom,<br><span class="zh">同样，对于贝壳、坚果、李子、梨、蝌蚪、蘑菇等固体</span>

a mountain peak, a kidney, a carrot, a tree-trunk, a bird, a bud, a lark, a ladybird, a bulrush, a bone.<br><span class="zh">山峰、肾脏、胡萝卜、树干、鸟、花蕾、云雀、瓢虫、香蒲、骨头</span>

From these he can go on to appreciate more complex forms or combinations of several forms.<br><span class="zh">从这些出发，他可以进而欣赏更复杂的形式或多种形式的组合</span>
