# 第 27 课：Nothing to Sell and Nothing to Buy

> [新概念英语 · 第三册 培养技能](/nce/ "返回目录")

Nothing to sell and nothing to buy<br><span class="zh">没有什么可以卖的，也没有什么可以买的。</span>

What is the most important thing for a tramp?<br><span class="zh">对于一个流浪者来说，最重要的是什么？</span>

It has been said that everyone lives by selling something.<br><span class="zh">有人说过，每个人都是通过出售某种东西来谋生的。</span>

In the light of this statement, teachers live by selling knowledge,<br><span class="zh">根据这一说法，教师的工作就是通过传授知识来谋生。</span>

philosophers by selling wisdom and priests by selling spiritual comfort.<br><span class="zh">哲学家通过传授智慧来谋生，而牧师则通过提供精神慰藉来获得收入。</span>

Though it may be possible to measure the value of material goods in terms of money,<br><span class="zh">虽然可以用金钱来衡量物质财富的价值，</span>

it is extremely difficult to estimate the true value of the services which people perform for us.<br><span class="zh">很难准确估计人们为我们提供的服务的真正价值。</span>

There are times when we would willingly give everything we possess to save our lives,<br><span class="zh">有时候，为了挽救自己的生命，我们甚至愿意放弃自己拥有的一切。</span>

yet we might grudge paying a surgeon a high fee for offering us precisely this service.<br><span class="zh">然而，我们可能会不愿意为外科医生支付高额的费用，仅仅因为他们为我们提供了这项服务。</span>

The conditions of society are such that skills have to be paid for in the same way that goods are paid for at a shop.<br><span class="zh">当前的社会状况决定了：技能的价值必须像商品在商店里被出售时那样，通过金钱来体现（即技能也需要通过金钱来获得）。</span>

Everyone has something to sell.<br><span class="zh">每个人都有东西可以出售。</span>

Tramps seem to be the only exception to this general rule.<br><span class="zh">流浪汉似乎是这条普遍规则的唯一例外。</span>

Beggars almost sell themselves as human beings to arouse the pity of passers-by.<br><span class="zh">乞丐们几乎是在“出卖自己的人格”，以激起路人的同情心。</span>

But real tramps are not beggars.<br><span class="zh">但是，真正的流浪者并不是乞丐。</span>

They have nothing to sell and require nothing from others.<br><span class="zh">他们没有什么可以出售的，也不需要从别人那里得到任何东西。</span>

In seeking independence, they do not sacrifice their human dignity.<br><span class="zh">在追求独立的过程中，他们并没有牺牲自己的人格尊严。</span>

A tramp may ask you for money, but he will never ask you to feel sorry for him.<br><span class="zh">一个流浪汉可能会向你乞讨钱财，但他永远不会要求你同情他。</span>

He has deliberately chosen to lead the life he leads and is fully aware of the consequences.<br><span class="zh">他自己是主动选择了这样的生活方式，并且完全清楚这样做会带来什么样的后果。</span>

He may never be sure where the next meal is coming from,<br><span class="zh">他可能永远都无法确定下一顿饭从哪里来（也就是说，他可能永远不知道自己下一顿饭的食物来源或来源地）。</span>

but he is free from the thousands of anxieties which afflict other people.<br><span class="zh">但他摆脱了那些困扰其他人的成千上万种焦虑情绪。</span>

His few material possessions make it possible for him to move from place to place with ease.<br><span class="zh">他仅有的一些物质财富使他能够轻松地从一个地方搬到另一个地方。</span>

By having to sleep in the open, he gets far closer to the world of nature than most of us ever do.<br><span class="zh">由于不得不在户外露宿，他比我们大多数人更接近大自然的世界。</span>

He may hunt, beg, or steal occasionally to keep himself alive;<br><span class="zh">他可能会偶尔通过打猎、乞讨或偷窃来维持生计。</span>

he may even, in times of real need, do a little work; but he will never sacrifice his freedom.<br><span class="zh">在真正需要的时候，他或许也会稍微工作一下；但他永远不会牺牲自己的自由。</span>

We often speak of tramps with contempt and put them in the same class as beggars,<br><span class="zh">我们常常轻蔑地看待流浪者，把他们和乞丐归为同一类，</span>

but how many of us can honestly say that we have not felt a little envious of their simple way of life and their freedom from care?<br><span class="zh">但又有多少人能坦诚地说，自己没有羡慕过他们简单的生活方式以及无忧无虑的生活状态呢？</span>
