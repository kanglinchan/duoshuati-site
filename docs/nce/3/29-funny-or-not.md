# 第 29 课：Funny or Not

> [新概念英语 · 第三册 培养技能](/nce/ "返回目录")

Funny or not?<br><span class="zh">好笑还是不好笑？</span>

What is the basis of 'sick' humour?<br><span class="zh">“病态”幽默（即那些令人不适、甚至反感的幽默）的依据是什么呢？</span>

Whether we find a joke funny or not largely depends on where we have been brought up.<br><span class="zh">我们是否觉得某个笑话好笑，很大程度上取决于我们的成长环境（即我们是在什么样的环境中长大的）。</span>

The sense of humour is mysteriously bound up with national characteristics.<br><span class="zh">幽默感似乎与一个国家的民族特性有着某种神秘的联系（或者说，幽默感是受民族特性影响的）。</span>

A Frenchman, for instance, might find it hard to laugh at a Russian joke.<br><span class="zh">例如，一个法国人可能很难被一个俄罗斯笑话逗笑。</span>

In the same way, a Russian might fail to see anything amusing in a joke<br><span class="zh">同样地，一个俄罗斯人可能根本看不出某个笑话有什么好笑的地方。</span>

which would make an Englishman laugh to tears.<br><span class="zh">而这个笑话却能让英国人笑出眼泪。</span>

Most funny stories are based on comic situations.<br><span class="zh">大多数有趣的故事都是基于一些滑稽、搞笑的情境创作的。</span>

In spite of national differences, certain funny situations have a universal appeal.<br><span class="zh">尽管存在文化差异，但某些有趣的情境却具有普遍的吸引力（即能够引起全球人们的共鸣）。</span>

No matter where you live,<br><span class="zh">无论你住在哪里，</span>

you would find it difficult not to laugh at, say, Charlie Chaplin's early films.<br><span class="zh">你很难不笑出来，比如说，看查理·卓别林的早期电影。</span>

However, a new type of humour, which stems largely from U.S., has recently come into fashion.<br><span class="zh">然而，最近一种新型的幽默风格开始流行起来，这种幽默主要源自美国。</span>

It is called 'sick humour'. Comedians base their jokes on tragic situations like violent death or serious accidents.<br><span class="zh">这被称为“病态幽默”（sick humour）。喜剧演员的笑料往往基于一些悲剧性场景，比如暴力死亡或严重事故。</span>

Many people find this sort of joke distasteful.<br><span class="zh">很多人觉得这种类型的笑话很令人反感（或：很无趣、很糟糕）。</span>

The following example of 'sick humour' will enable you to judge for yourself.<br><span class="zh">以下这个“病态幽默”（sick humour）的例子，可以让你自己判断一下这种幽默风格到底如何。</span>

A man who had broken his right leg was taken to hospital a few weeks before Christmas.<br><span class="zh">一名右腿骨折的男子在圣诞节前几周被送往医院。</span>

From the moment he arrived there,<br><span class="zh">从他到达那里的那一刻起，</span>

he kept on pestering his doctor to tell him when he would be able to go home.<br><span class="zh">他一直缠着医生，想知道自己什么时候可以回家。</span>

He dreaded having to spend Christmas in hospital.<br><span class="zh">他非常害怕不得不在医院里度过圣诞节。</span>

Though the doctor did his best, the patient's recovery was slow.<br><span class="zh">尽管医生尽了最大努力，患者的康复进程仍然很缓慢。</span>

On Christmas day, the man still had his right leg in plaster.<br><span class="zh">在圣诞节那天，那个人的右腿仍然打着石膏。</span>

He spent a miserable day in bed thinking of all the fun he was missing.<br><span class="zh">他在床上度过了痛苦的一天，一直在想着自己错过的所有乐趣。</span>

The following day, however, the doctor consoled him by telling him<br><span class="zh">然而，第二天医生安慰他说：</span>

that his chances of being able to leave hospital in time for New Year celebrations were good.<br><span class="zh">他按时出院、能够参加新年庆祝活动的几率很大。</span>

The man took heart and sure enough, on New Year's Eve he was able to hobble along to a party.<br><span class="zh">那男人重新振作起来，果然在除夕夜时，他还能一瘸一拐地赶到参加派对。</span>

To compensate for his unpleasant experiences in hospital,<br><span class="zh">为了弥补他在医院里所经历的不愉快经历，</span>

the man drank a little more than was good for him.<br><span class="zh">那个人喝得有点过量了。</span>

In the process, he enjoyed himself thoroughly and kept telling everybody how much he hated hospitals.<br><span class="zh">在这个过程中，他玩得非常开心，还不停地告诉所有人他有多讨厌医院。</span>

He was still mumbling something about hospitals at the end of the party when he slipped on a piece of ice and broke his left leg.<br><span class="zh">派对结束时，他还在嘟囔着关于医院的事情；突然，他踩到一块冰，摔倒了，导致左腿骨折。</span>
