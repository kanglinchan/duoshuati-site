# 第 55 课：From the Earth Greetings

> [新概念英语 · 第三册 培养技能](/nce/ "返回目录")

From the earth: Greetings<br><span class="zh">来自地球的问候：</span>

Which life forms are most likely to develop on a distant planet?<br><span class="zh">哪些生命形式最有可能在遥远的行星上出现？</span>

Recent developments in astronomy have made it possible to detect planets in our own Milky Way and in other galaxies.<br><span class="zh">天文学的最新发展使得我们能够探测到我们自己的银河系以及其他星系中的行星。</span>

This is a major achievement because, in relative terms, planets are very small and do not emit light.<br><span class="zh">这是一个重大成就，因为在相对而言，行昂数体积很小，而且自身并不 emitting 光。</span>

Finding planets is proving hard enough, but finding life on them will prove infinitely more difficult.<br><span class="zh">这是一个重大成就，因为相对于其他天体而言，行星体积非常小，且本身并不发光。</span>

The first question to answer is whether a planet can actually support life.<br><span class="zh">找到行星已经相当困难，而在行星上寻找生命则更加艰巨。</span>

In our own solar system, for example, Venus is far to hot and Mars is far too cold to support life.<br><span class="zh">首先需要回答的问题是：行星是否真的能够支持生命存在。</span>

Only the Earth provides ideal conditions, and even here it has taken more than four billion years for plant and animal life to evolve.<br><span class="zh">以我们的太阳系为例，金星温度过高，而火星则过于寒冷，都无法维持生命。</span>

Whether a planet can support life depends on the size and brightness of its star, that is its 'sun'.<br><span class="zh">只有地球具备了理想的生存条件；即便如此，植物和动物的进化也花费了超过40亿年的时间。</span>

Imagine a star up to twenty times larger, brighter and hotter than our own sun.<br><span class="zh">一个行星能否支持生命，取决于其恒星（即它的“太阳”）的大小和亮度。</span>

A planet would have to be a very long way from it to be capable of supporting life.<br><span class="zh">想象一颗比我们的太阳大20倍、亮20倍、热20倍的恒星。那么，行星必须距离它非常遥远，才有可能支持生命。</span>

Alternatively, if the star were small,<br><span class="zh">或者，如果那颗恒星很小，</span>

the life-supporting planet would have to have a close orbit round it and also provide the perfect conditions for life forms to develop.<br><span class="zh">这颗能够维持生命的行星必须拥有一个靠近该恒星的轨道，并且必须提供适合生命形式发展的完美条件。</span>

But how would we find such a planet?<br><span class="zh">但是，我们该如何找到这样一颗行星呢？</span>

At present, there is no telescope in existence that is capable of detecting the presence of life.<br><span class="zh">目前，还没有任何现有的望远镜能够探测到生命的存在。</span>

The development of such a telescope will be one of the great astronomical projects of the 21st century.<br><span class="zh">开发这样的望远镜将是21世纪最重要的天文项目之一。</span>

It is impossible to look for life on another planet using earth-based telescopes.<br><span class="zh">使用地球上的望远镜，是不可能在其他行星上寻找生命的。</span>

Our own warm atmosphere and the heat generated by the telescope<br><span class="zh">我们自身的温暖环境，以及望远镜所产生的热量……</span>

would make it impossible to detect objects as small as planets.<br><span class="zh">这将使得检测像行星这样微小的物体变得不可能。</span>

Even a telescope in orbit round the earth, like the very successful Hubble telescope,<br><span class="zh">即使是像哈勃望远镜这样非常成功的、位于地球轨道上的望远镜，</span>

would not be suitable because of the dust particles in our solar system.<br><span class="zh">由于太阳系中存在大量的尘埃颗粒，这种方法并不适用。</span>

A telescope would have to be as far away as the planet Jupiter to look for life in outer space,<br><span class="zh">望远镜必须远到像木星那样远的地方，才能在外太空寻找生命，</span>

because the dust becomes thinner the further we travel towards the outer edges of our own solar system.<br><span class="zh">因为我们越往太阳系外围走，尘埃就越稀薄。</span>

Once we detected a planet, we would have to find a way of blotting out the light from its star,<br><span class="zh">一旦我们发现了一颗行星，我们就必须想办法遮挡掉来自该行星恒星的光线。</span>

so that we would be able to 'see' the planet properly and analyse its atmosphere.<br><span class="zh">这样我们就能清楚地“看到”这颗行星，并分析其大气层了。</span>

In the first instance, we would be looking for plant life, rather than 'little green men'.<br><span class="zh">首先，我们寻找的是植物生命，而不是所谓的“小绿人”。</span>

The life forms most likely to develop on a planet would be bacteria.<br><span class="zh">最有可能在行星上生存的生命形式是细菌。</span>

It is bacteria that have generated the oxygen we breathe on earth.<br><span class="zh">正是细菌产生了我们地球上所呼吸的氧气。</span>

For most of the earth's history they have been the only form of life on our planet.<br><span class="zh">在地球历史的大部分时间里，它们都是地球上唯一的生命形式。</span>

As Earth-dwellers, we always cherish the hope that we will be visited by little green men and that we will be able to communicate with them.<br><span class="zh">作为地球人，我们始终怀揣着这样的希望：有一天，外星的小绿人会来到我们身边，我们能够与他们进行交流。</span>

But this hope is always in the realms of science fiction.<br><span class="zh">然而，这种希望始终只存在于科幻作品的范畴之中。</span>

If we were able to discover lowly forms of life like bacteria on another planet, it would completely change our view of ourselves.<br><span class="zh">如果我们能够在另一颗行星上发现像细菌这样的简单生命形式，那将彻底改变我们对自己的认知。</span>

As Daniel Goldin of NASA observed, 'Finding life elsewhere would change everything.<br><span class="zh">正如NASA的丹尼尔·戈尔丁所说：“在其它地方发现生命将会改变一切。”</span>

No human endeavour or thought would be unchanged by it.'<br><span class="zh">没有任何人类的努力或思想会因此而保持不变。</span>
